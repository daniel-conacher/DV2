Community gardens across Australia

Growing more than food

Australia's community gardens are spread unevenly, from school plots in Tasmania to five-hectare farms in Newcastle. Surveys show they are getting busier, giving away more food and leaving most members feeling better.

1Where are the gardens?

More than 600 gardens are listed in the national directory, concentrated along the east coast and in capital cities.

2Where are gardens most common?

Per resident, Tasmania lists the most gardens and Queensland the fewest.

Zooming into council areas shows the pattern inside each state. Inner-city councils such as Port Phillip, Yarra and the City of Sydney have the most gardens per resident among the big cities. Some remote councils look dark only because one garden among a few thousand people produces a high rate.

3How big are they?

Half of the gardens that reported a size are under 700 m². A handful of city farms cover several hectares.

4Gardens are getting busier

In 2023 only 2 surveyed gardens had more than 100 visitors a week. In 2024, 17 did.

5What gardeners get back

Every year, more than 80% of respondents say their health and wellbeing improved after joining.

6Feeding the neighbourhood

A growing share of gardens give produce away to food banks, soup kitchens and charities. CGA's 2023 report links the rise to cost-of-living pressure.

7Low-impact by design

Nine in ten gardens mulch their beds every year. The one real shift is fewer gardens strongly encouraging organic growing.

8Running on goodwill

More gardens fund themselves through plot fees and sausage sizzles, while funding for paid staff has almost disappeared.

9Reading the data carefully

The surveys are self-selected, the mix of states changes each year, and the national directory misses many gardens.
